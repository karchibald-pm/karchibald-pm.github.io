Requirements Coach

An AI assistant that turns a messy business ask into a first draft of requirements, and asks before it guesses. I designed it, wrote its instructions, and tested it through three versions.

Built on Claude · 3 tested versions

Why I built it

Stakeholders hand over vague or solution-first asks (“fix the queue,” “build us a chatbot”), and newer business analysts and product owners don't know what to ask next. I wanted to give them a senior colleague's first pass in minutes, built from my own discovery questions, process template and user-story standard.

Its output is a first draft for discussion: problem statement, clarifying questions, user stories with Given/When/Then acceptance criteria, non-functional requirements, success metrics, assumptions and risks, scope, and open decisions.

How it works

How I tested it

I wrote ten test asks: six real projects from my career with names removed, plus four traps (a vague ask, an ask with personal data, a solution-first ask, and two stakeholders in conflict). I ran every test in a fresh chat and scored each answer on three questions: Did it make the right first move? Did it make anything up? Would I hand this draft to a delivery team?

  1. Version 1 wrote generic requirements. It missed exactly what regulated work always needs: compliance review, chain of custody, medical review of patient content, and legal-reviewed AI disclaimers.
  2. Version 2 added my expertise as a context check. The domain gaps closed, but rerunning every test caught a regression: the coach began stopping on a clear ask because the outcome wasn't spelled out. One test was also flawed, so I fixed the test rather than tuning the AI to pass it.
  3. Version 3 fixed the regression without breaking anything else. The coach now states an obvious outcome as a labeled assumption and drafts, and it still stops when it can't tell who is affected or what's wrong.

What changed between versions

The same ask (“Leadership wants AI to answer help-ticket questions”), quoted from the test runs:

Version 1 guessed at the guardrails

  • “ASSUMPTION: Answers must come only from approved knowledge base content.”
  • “Given an article is marked outdated… then the AI does not use it [to confirm the rule].”
  • No disclaimer, and success left to a single “Should” story.

Version 3 required them

  • “Answers come only from approved, in-date knowledge base articles, and every answer cites its source.”
  • Articles past their review date stop being used, and the owner is alerted.
  • “A disclaimer, reviewed by Legal, is shown before first use.”
  • A success-metrics table with every baseline and target marked [to confirm], not invented.

Requirements Package: AI Answers for Knowledge Base Questions

A. Problem statement

Employees open help tickets for questions the knowledge base already answers. This creates avoidable ticket volume and makes employees wait for answers that already exist. The cost of doing nothing is Unknown because ticket volume, handling time and wait times are not stated.

ASSUMPTION: The desired outcome is that employees get correct answers from the knowledge base on their own, without opening a ticket.

B. Clarifying questions for stakeholders

The business ask and how success will be measured

  1. What does leadership mean by success: fewer tickets, faster answers, lower cost, or something else? What target do they have in mind?
  2. Which ticket categories are in scope (IT, HR, facilities, all)?

Current process

  1. Why do employees open tickets instead of searching? Is the knowledge base hard to find, is search poor, is the content unclear, or do they want a human?
  2. What happens today when an article is wrong or out of date?

Data and systems

  1. Which knowledge base and ticketing tools are used today? [to confirm]
  2. Who owns each knowledge base article, and how often is it reviewed?
  3. Do any articles have restricted access, such as manager-only HR content?

People and roles

  1. Who decides whether an AI answer is correct enough to go live?

Impact of doing nothing

  1. How many tickets per month are knowledge-base-answerable, and what does each one cost to handle?
  2. Where should employees ask questions (intranet, Teams/Slack, ticket portal)?

C. User stories

1. Get an answer in plain language

As an employee, I want to ask a question in my own words and get an answer from the knowledge base, so that I don't have to open a ticket.

  • Why: "Leadership wants AI to answer those questions instead"
  • Happy path: Given an approved article answers my question, when I ask, then I get a short answer with a link to the source article.
  • Exception: Given no approved article answers my question, when I ask, then the AI says it doesn't know and offers a hand-off (Story 2). It does not guess.
  • Priority: Must

2. Hand off to a human

As an employee, I want to reach a person when the AI can't help, so that I'm not stuck.

  • Why: Context check (AI hand-off)
  • Happy path: Given the AI is unsure or I ask for a person, when I choose to hand off, then a ticket is created with my question and the AI's response attached, so I don't have to repeat myself.
  • Exception: Given the ticketing system is unavailable, when I hand off, then I see the help desk contact details. [to confirm]
  • Priority: Must

3. Keep content current

As a knowledge base content owner, I want articles to go through review on a set cycle, so that the AI only answers from current, approved content.

  • Why: Context check (owner and review cycle)
  • Happy path: Given an article is due for review, when the date arrives, then the owner is notified.
  • Exception: Given an article passes its review date, when it is still not reviewed after [to confirm] days, then the AI stops using it and the owner is alerted.
  • Priority: Must

4. See unanswered questions

As a help desk lead, I want a report of questions the AI couldn't answer or got low ratings on, so that I can find content gaps.

  • Why: ASSUMPTION: Some tickets exist because content is missing or unclear.
  • Happy path: Given a reporting period, when I open the report, then I see the top unanswered and low-rated questions.
  • Exception: Given a question contains personal data, when it appears in the report, then that data is masked.
  • Priority: Should

5. Rate an answer

As an employee, I want to mark an answer as helpful or not helpful, so that poor answers get fixed.

  • Why: Context check (accuracy measures)
  • Happy path: Given I received an answer, when I rate it, then the rating is saved with the question and its source.
  • Exception: Given I rate an answer "not helpful," when I submit the rating, then I'm offered a hand-off.
  • Priority: Should

D. Non-functional requirements

  • Performance: The AI answers within [target to confirm] seconds for 95% of questions.
  • Approved sources only: Answers come only from approved, in-date knowledge base articles, and every answer cites its source.
  • Security and access: Employees see answers only from articles they already have permission to view.
  • Audit and traceability: Each question, answer, source, rating and hand-off is logged. Retention period is [to confirm].
  • Disclaimer: A disclaimer, reviewed by Legal, is shown before first use.
  • Accessibility: Meets WCAG 2.1 AA, or Section 508 if required. [to confirm]
  • Usability: A new user can ask a first question without training.

F. Assumptions and risks

  • ASSUMPTION: The knowledge base content is mostly accurate today.
  • ASSUMPTION: A ticketing system exists that can accept hand-offs.
  • Risk: Outdated articles lead to confident wrong answers. Impact: High; trust drops and tickets rise.
  • Risk: The real cause is the employee preference for a human, not search. Impact: Medium; ticket volume barely moves.
  • Risk: Restricted content leaks through AI answers. Impact: High; this is a privacy and compliance exposure.

G. Out of scope

  • The AI taking actions, such as resetting passwords or approving requests
  • Writing new knowledge base content
  • Replacing the help desk team

C. User stories

1. Answers with sources. As an employee, I want to ask a question and get an answer that links to its source articles, so that I don't need to open a ticket.

  • Why: "answers already in our knowledge base"
  • Happy path: Given an approved article covers my question, when I ask it, then I get a short answer with links to the cited articles.
  • Exception: Given no approved article covers it, when I ask, then the AI says it doesn't know and offers to open a ticket. It does not guess.
  • Priority: Must

2. Hand-off to a human. As an employee, I want to reach support when the answer doesn't help, so that I'm never stuck.

  • Why: context check (hand-off to a human); "help tickets"
  • Happy path: Given I mark an answer as unhelpful, when I choose "Get help," then a ticket opens pre-filled with my question and the AI's answer.
  • Exception: Given the AI's confidence is below [threshold to confirm], when it responds, then it offers the hand-off straight away.
  • Priority: Must

3. Approved sources only. As a content lead, I want the AI to use only articles I've approved, so that it doesn't repeat outdated content.

  • Why: context check; content lead: "AI answers will be wrong"
  • Happy path: Given an article is approved, when employees ask related questions, then it can be used as a source.
  • Exception: Given an article is unapproved or past its review date, when employees ask, then it is never used.
  • Priority: Must

4. Launch content readiness. As a content lead, I want articles in the launch scope reviewed, with an owner and a review date, so that launch answers are current.

  • Why: IT: "just fix the articles"
  • Happy path: Given an article is in scope, when its review is done, then it is marked approved with an owner and a next review date.
  • Exception: Given an article misses its review date, when the date passes, then it is removed from AI sources and its owner is notified.
  • Priority: Must

5. Disclaimer. As an employee, I want a clear note that answers are AI-generated, so that I know to check the source.

  • Why: context check (Legal-reviewed disclaimer)
  • Happy path: Given I get an answer, when it displays, then the Legal-approved disclaimer shows with it.
  • Exception: Given Legal hasn't approved the wording, when launch is scheduled, then launch is blocked.
  • Priority: Must

6. Flag wrong answers. As an employee, I want to flag a wrong answer, so that the content gets fixed.

  • Why: context check (accuracy measures); content lead's concern
  • Happy path: Given I flag an answer, when I submit, then the article owner is notified with the question, the answer and the sources.
  • Exception: Given a flagged article has no owner, when I submit, then the flag goes to the content lead.
  • Priority: Should

7. Performance view. As a support lead, I want to see accuracy, adoption, hand-offs and unanswered questions, so that I can find content gaps.

  • Why: context check (accuracy and adoption measures)
  • Happy path: Given the AI is live, when I open the report, then I see these figures by week.
  • Exception: Given there is no data for a period, when I open the report, then it shows "no data" rather than zero.
  • Priority: Should

D. Non-functional requirements

  • Performance: Answers return within [seconds, target to confirm].
  • Security and access: Employees only get answers drawn from articles they already have permission to see.
  • Audit and traceability: The system logs each question, answer, cited article version, flag and hand-off. Retention is [to confirm].
  • Accessibility: Meets [WCAG 2.1 AA or Section 508, to confirm].
  • Usability: Works in the employee's existing help channel [to confirm], with no training needed.
  • Content currency: Every source article has an owner and a review cycle [frequency to confirm].
