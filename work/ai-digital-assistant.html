Designing an AI Digital Assistant for Clinical Trials

I led the requirements and partnered on the user journey for a lifelike AI digital assistant that helps patients find and understand clinical trials, taking it from rough specs to a 100+ requirement baseline and an approved MVP design.

Designed to MVP; paused before launch

Company
Global pharma company, oncology
My role
Sr. Manager, Business Analyst / Product Manager; helped shape the product in Phase 0 alongside senior product leaders; led 2 contracted business analysts
Partners
A digital-human technology partner and a consulting partner
Timeline
2024
Users
Cancer patients and caregivers (originally healthcare professionals)
Product
AI digital assistant with a lifelike face and voice, helping patients explore clinical trials on mobile and desktop

The problem

Clinical trials need participants to succeed, and successful trials are how new treatments reach patients. But patients and doctors often struggle to find and understand the trials available to them, which slows enrollment.

The program set out to change that with a lifelike AI digital assistant that could talk people through their options with precision and compassion, starting with a single cancer type as the MVP.

My approach

Alongside our product leaders and consulting partner, I helped shape a new kind of product in its first phase, and led the requirements that turned rough specs into a baseline the team could build from and trust.

  1. Built the requirements framework for a new kind of product. A lifelike AI assistant needed more than system requirements, so I defined what had to be documented: the assistant’s and the user’s experience, legal, AI behavior and non-functional requirements. I drafted the original framework, my business analysts refined it with stakeholders, and it became the baseline the team vetted together. I also partnered with UX on the end-to-end journey, validating each step and identifying the gaps.
  2. Reset the plan when the audience changed. When CX research showed healthcare professionals wouldn’t use the assistant and the real need was with patients, I made the case to leadership that this was a new product, not a reskin, and secured three more months to do it right. We rebuilt the journeys for patients and caregivers, with happy and unhappy paths written in plain, empathetic language.
  3. Shaped the conversation design. I worked with our consulting partner on the voice flow, the if/then actions that guide the assistant’s conversations with patients, and joined UX design reviews to keep the experience aligned with the requirements.
  4. Built responsible AI in from the start. As a team, we identified where patient data would appear, talked through how patient conversations would be stored, and worked with Legal on the boundaries for medical guidance.
  5. Baselined 100+ requirements. I led my team in baselining more than 100 requirements while legal review ran in parallel, so the work was protected and ready to resume at any point.

How it works

What the MVP covered

  • Answers to common questions about cancer clinical trials, on mobile and desktop
  • Links to trial search and pre-screening
  • The nurse navigator phone number for a human conversation
  • Feedback captured when a patient leaves, plus baseline metrics: pre-screen completion, where people drop off, and satisfaction

Later phases were planned on a Now / Next / Later roadmap: personalization, secure capture of personal information, more cancer types, trial matching, live-agent handoff, accessibility, more languages, and recognizing adverse-event language to route people to the safety reporting portal.

What happened

  • Designed to MVP: more than 100 requirements baselined, the patient journey and voice flow mapped, and the project brought to the demo-approval stage.
  • Ready to resume: the program paused before launch, but with 100+ requirements baselined, the work can pick up right where it left off.

What I learned

  • System requirements tell you what a product must do; the user journey tells you what it must get right.
  • When your customer changes, your requirements change with it. The pivot to patients was a new product, and treating it that way earned the time to do it well.
  • For patient-facing AI, legal is a design partner, not a final checkpoint. Bringing legal in early shapes what the AI can say, how data is handled and where the guardrails sit, before anything is built.
