Case study 4 · Agentic AI discovery

Mapping a Hidden Process for AI Agents

I designed and led the discovery that turned an undocumented supply-chain planning process into an AI-ready blueprint, so the AI team knew exactly which steps to automate and which needed human judgment.

Handed off; automation built

Company
Global pharma company, global supply chain (new product launches)
My role
Sr. Manager, Digital Business Analyst; brought in to lead discovery
Worked with
Small- and large-molecule supply planning leads, and the AI team
Timeline
About one month of sessions, readout in June 2024
Product
Discovery for agentic AI and intelligent automation in launch planning

The problem

Bringing new medicines to market means planning many product launches at once, across several systems and spreadsheets with disconnected inputs. One of the most critical decisions is sourcing: which manufacturing sites can supply the volumes needed, when to scale up to larger equipment, and when to scale out to an external manufacturer, while many launches compete for the same materials.

Leadership wanted AI to predict and respond to launch disruptions. But the sourcing process had never been written down. It lived in a few experts' heads and spreadsheets, so the knowledge was hard to share, scale or automate. You can't automate what you can't see.

My approach

I designed a seven-step discovery method and ran every session myself, twice a week for about a month, with the small- and large-molecule leads.

  1. Observe. The experts walked me through their real work before I asked a single question.
  2. Facilitate. Structured sessions, with an AI assistant (Copilot) capturing and summarizing each step and the reasoning behind it.
  3. Decompose. A functional decomposition diagram with traceable IDs, so every step could be referenced later.
  4. Unpack. A template for every step: why it exists, inputs, triggers, tools, documents, conditions, exceptions, outputs and scenarios.
  5. Map. The small- and large-molecule processes side by side in Miro, with two disruptors overlaid: a forecast change and a timeline change.
  6. Focus. I facilitated sessions with the business leads, walking them through the map so they could see exactly which steps could be automated. They chose the two highest-value scenarios, Scale Up and Scale Out, and marked which steps need human judgment.
  7. Hand off. A job aid and recorded walkthroughs of every step went to the AI team, so they could design the automation without starting from scratch.

How it works

Results

  • Documented for the first time: the sourcing process, end to end, for both small and large molecules.
  • AI-ready: the AI team received everything it needed to design the automation, with human-judgment steps clearly marked.
  • Built: after my handoff, the AI team built automation from the blueprint.
  • Prioritized: the readout made this one of several AI opportunities the business chose to explore.

What I learned

You can't automate what you can't see. The fastest path to AI is often a clear map of the current state. And the best way to decide what to automate is to let the business see the whole process in front of them, then make the choice with them.
