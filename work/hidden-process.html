Mapping a Hidden Process for AI Agents

An AI-ready blueprint of an undocumented supply-chain sourcing process for new product launches. Built to show the AI team exactly which steps to automate and which need human judgment.

Handed off; automation built

Company
Global pharma company, global supply chain (new product launches)

Sr. Manager, Business Analyst / Product Manager; brought in to lead discovery
Worked with
Small- and large-molecule supply planning leads, and the AI team
Timeline
About one month of sessions, readout in June 2024
Product
Discovery for agentic AI and intelligent automation in launch planning

Details have been generalized and confidential information removed to honor non-disclosure agreements.

Overview

Bringing new medicines to market means managing a large portfolio of product launches at once, each with multiple stages and largely manual, disconnected inputs. One of the most critical decisions is sourcing: which sites can supply the volumes needed and how to adjust capacity, while many launches compete for the same materials. Many outside factors can disrupt these plans, with knock-on effects on every stage that follows. Leadership wanted AI to predict and respond to launch disruptions, but the sourcing process had never been written down. It lived in a few experts' heads and spreadsheets. You can't automate what you can't see.

AI hypothesis

We believed

If the hidden sourcing process is mapped step by step, with the steps that need human judgment clearly marked, AI agents can take on the repeatable work and help planners respond to launch disruptions faster.

Product lifecycle

Top contributions and results

From discovery to handoff: what I did in each phase, and what it produced.

1

Discovery

Making the hidden process visible
Top contributions
  • Designed a seven-step discovery method and ran every session, twice a week for about a month
  • Observed the experts' real work before asking questions, with an AI assistant (Copilot) capturing each step and its reasoning
  • Documented the sourcing process step by step
  • Built a functional decomposition with traceable IDs, and a template for every step: inputs, triggers, tools, conditions, exceptions and outputs
  • Mapped the small- and large-molecule processes side by side in Miro, with forecast and timeline disruptors overlaid
Results
  • Sourcing process documented end to end for the first time, for both small and large molecules
2

Prioritization

Deciding where AI helps
Top contributions
  • Walked the business leads through the map so they could see which steps could be automated
  • Facilitated their choice of the two highest-value scenarios
  • Marked, with the leads, which steps need human judgment
Results
  • Two highest-value scenarios chosen by the business
  • Human-in-the-loop steps clearly marked
  • One of several AI opportunities the business chose to explore
3

Handoff

Blueprint to the AI team
Top contributions
  • Delivered a job aid and recorded walkthroughs of every step, so the AI team could design the automation without starting from scratch
Results
  • The AI team built automation from the blueprint
  • Projected ~10–15% faster time-to-launch
  • Bravo award from the Sr. Director

How it works: from hidden process to AI-ready blueprint

Blueprint delivered

DeliverableWhat it gave the AI team
Functional decompositionEvery step of the sourcing process, with traceable IDs so each one could be referenced later.
Step templatesFor each step: why it exists, inputs, triggers, tools, documents, conditions, exceptions, outputs and scenarios.
Side-by-side process mapsSmall- and large-molecule processes in Miro, with forecast and timeline disruptors overlaid.
Job aid and walkthroughsRecorded walkthroughs of every step, with human-judgment steps marked for the chosen scenarios.

What I learned

You can't automate what you can't see. The fastest path to AI is often a clear map of the current state. And the best way to decide what to automate is to let the business see the whole process in front of them, then make the choice with them.
