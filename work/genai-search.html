GenAI Search for 83,000 Employees

I owned the employee experience as a global pharma company added AI-generated answers to its employee portal search, a conversational AI feature to its existing virtual agent, and AI chat summaries that help agents update case notes, so 83,000 colleagues in 13 countries could get answers before opening a ticket.

Three GenAI features on ServiceNow (Now Assist): search answers with cited sources and conversational requests for colleagues, plus chat summaries for support agents

The problem

As leaders, our job is to find ways to save money, remove extra steps and make work easier for colleagues. When we reviewed our ticket volume and the types of tickets being raised, a pattern stood out: many were from colleagues who simply couldn't find the information they needed.

Leadership decided to enhance the portal's existing features with AI to fix this, helping colleagues find answers on their own and reducing avoidable tickets.

My approach

My role was to understand why colleagues couldn't find the information they needed, and how the new AI-enhanced search would solve that.

  1. Found the real blocker: content structure. Early on, we found that our existing content wasn't in the structured format the new AI search needed to work well. Articles had to be converted to a standardized template.
  2. Sized the fix against the timeline. I worked with the Content Lead to understand the level of effort her team needed to convert all the content, and whether it was feasible within the timeline we had set.
  3. Partnered on the cleanup. The Content Lead worked with content authors to update thousands of articles to the new template. With the content ready, we turned on the feature and tested it.
  4. Built in trust and testing. As a team, we worked with Legal on AI disclaimers, and answers come only from approved articles with their sources shown. I was one of the leads for user acceptance testing. I helped build the testing tracker, which mapped what to test and the impact on each platform area my portal supports. A vendor supplied test resources, and our team held them accountable by reviewing their test scripts to make sure they covered the right users and roles and tested the right things.
  5. Supported a smooth rollout. My team and I supported the Change Management and Training leads on rollout communications and training. With our digital partners, we came up with a “try the virtual agent first” approach to raise adoption.

As the Business Owner and Product Manager, managing the business portfolio and product roadmap, I partnered with the Digital Product Manager, whose team configured the AI. ServiceNow's Now Assist came with a menu of ready-made AI capabilities; together we chose the ones that fit our colleagues' needs, and his team configured them rather than building custom ones. Using what the platform already offered was a large part of why we launched on schedule.

Results

  • Delivered: all three AI features launched on schedule for 83,000 colleagues in 13 countries.
  • Delivered: thousands of knowledge articles cleaned up and standardized before launch, through my partnership with the Content Lead and content owners.
  • Recognized: my director credited the program's “three launches in record time” to my “knowledge, input, support and execution.”

What I learned

AI search is only as good as how its content is structured. The AI could only answer what the knowledge base said, and only as well as the content was organized, so content readiness had to be a launch requirement, not a cleanup project for later.
